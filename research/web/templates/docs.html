How TruePrice Works: Formulas & Defense Architecture

A straightforward technical guide to the exact mathematical formulas, clustering logic, and reputation updates that power the platform.

1. The Big Picture & System Philosophy

In crowdsourced price tracking, malicious users or botnets can easily submit falsified prices (e.g. promoting fake discounts). If every user's vote is counted equally, attackers can easily outnumber real users.

TruePrice solves this by weighting each submission by the user's reputation score (bounded between 0.10 and 0.90). Fresh clients begin at neutral 0.50. When internal consensus is untrusted or divided, the system triggers authoritative validation against ground truth.

Key Principle: External validation acts as training wheels. At first, many validations occur. But as honest users prove themselves and gain trust, validations plummet, and the system runs autonomously.

2. Price Clustering Formula (Relative Tolerance)

Before consensus can be computed, incoming price submissions must be grouped into price clusters. Two prices \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) belong to the same cluster if their gap is within a relative tolerance (default: 2%):

Relative Tolerance Formula
gap = (pnext - pprev) / pprev ≤ 0.02 (2.0%)

Why Relative Gap instead of Absolute Dollars?
A $2.00 difference on a $10.00 book is a huge 20% error, but on a $2,000.00 television it is negligible (0.1%). Relative tolerance scales automatically across all price tags.

Clustering Algorithm: Sort submissions ascending by price. Walk the list sequentially. If the gap from the previous price is ≤ 2%, append to the current cluster. Otherwise, begin a new cluster.

3. Reputation-Weighted Consensus & Weighted Median

Each cluster's voting strength is the sum of reputations of all participating clients in that cluster:

Weighted Cluster Support & Confidence
Support(Cluster) = ∑ Reputation(Clienti)

Confidence = Winning_Cluster_Support / Total_Support_All_Clusters

Winning Cluster Selection:

  • The cluster with the highest weighted support wins.
  • Ties are broken deterministically by higher unique client count, then by lower representative price.
  • Ten attackers at reputation 0.10 have a total support of 1.0. Just two honest users at reputation 0.80 have support 1.6, outvoting all ten attackers.

Representative Price (Weighted Median):
The canonical crowd price is the weighted median of observations in the winning cluster. Unlike a simple average, the weighted median cannot be skewed by extreme internal outliers.

4. Validation Triggers (The Safety Guardrails)

The platform refuses to accept crowd prices blindly. External validation against ground truth is automatically triggered if any of these conditions occur:

1. Untrusted Crowd
trusted_ratio < 0.50

Fewer than 50% of active users have reputation > 0.5 (occurs early on Day 0).

2. Weak Consensus / Agreement
confidence < 0.80
or trusted_agreement < 0.80

Overall crowd confidence or agreement among trusted submissions fell below 80%.

3. Cluster Tie
|Support1 - Support2| < 1e-9

Top two clusters are tied. Internal consensus cannot break the deadlock.

When validation triggers: The true market price authoritatively overrides the crowd, and all user observations for that hour are graded against it.

5. Reputation Adjustment: Bounded-Proportional Math

Client reputations are strictly bounded in [0.10, 0.90] and start at neutral 0.50. The update model is strength-based and bounded-proportional:

Step 1: Compute Update Magnitude
normalized_strength = max(0.0, 2.0 × (consensus_strength - 0.5))
magnitude = base_reward (0.05) + strength_factor (0.10) × normalized_strength
• Split 50/50 consensus → magnitude = 0.05
• Unanimous consensus → magnitude = 0.15
Step 2: Apply Bounded-Proportional Update
If Submission is Correct (Reward):
  scale = (0.90 - current_reputation) / (0.90 - 0.10)
  delta = magnitude × scale
  new_reputation = current_reputation + delta

If Submission is Incorrect (Penalty):
  scale = (current_reputation - 0.10) / (0.90 - 0.10)
  delta = magnitude × scale
  new_reputation = current_reputation - delta

Result is clamped to [0.10, 0.90].

Why This Specific Math Defeats Attackers:

  • Trust is Easy to Lose: A high-reputation sleeper cell at 0.80 has a penalty scale of (0.80 - 0.10) / 0.80 = 0.875. When they submit bad data, their reputation drops violently, crushing them down to 0.10 in just 2-3 ticks.
  • Trust is Hard to Rebuild: A bad client at 0.10 gains incrementally and cannot regain voting power through a few lucky guesses.
  • Forgiving Honest Human Error: Honest users who make occasional 10% errors take a small, proportional dip, but quickly recover on subsequent correct ticks.
